When H₀ is true

Keep the real mean at 100. The test still rejects about α of the time. Each of those is a Type I error, a false alarm.

When H₀ is false

Move the real mean to 106. Now the share of rejections is the power. It grows with a bigger effect, a larger n or a larger α.

Reading a p-value

The orange area is the chance of a result at least this far from 100 if H₀ were true. A small p-value means the data would be surprising under H₀.
