The method is 95% reliable

About 95% of intervals catch μ in the long run. Any single interval either contains μ or it does not. The 95% describes the procedure, not one interval.

Confidence costs width

Switch from 90% to 99% and the intervals get wider. Larger n makes them narrower, roughly like 1/√n.

Assumptions matter

With the right-skewed population and a small sample (try n = 5), the share that catch μ drops below the target. With larger n it recovers.
